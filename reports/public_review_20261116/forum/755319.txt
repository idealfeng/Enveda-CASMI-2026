Comment: dauriel

I get the frustration, but I'd push back a little on "what's the point." A fork's public score isn't a medal yet, and in this
    competition the gap between the two is bigger than usual.

Two things people forking at .479 may not have checked:


The rules on external assets are strict here. The host has said pretrained weights are only allowed when the original training code and the training data are both permitted, and that NIST-derived content is banned outright. Several of the popular forks chain together checkpoints and spectral libraries whose provenance nobody downstream has verified. If one of those pieces fails the host's check, every team that forked it is exposed to the same disqualification, not just the original author.


Forks rise and fall together on the private board. A few hundred teams sit on the exact same public score because they are    the same submission. Whatever the private split does to that notebook, it does to all of them at once. The public     leaderboard makes it look like a wall of medals; the private one will treat them as a single entry with a few hundred copies.
