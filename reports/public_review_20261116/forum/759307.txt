I wanted to share a lightweight, custom PyTorch architecture I've been experimenting with for MS2 spectral retrieval, along with a fast baseline notebook that runs end-to-end in ~2 minutes on GPU without internet access.

💡 Core Architectural Ideas

Instead of standard additive MLP layers or heavy transformers, SpectralHybridEncoder combines two custom inductive biases tailored to mass spectrometry:


Parallel Division-Multiplication Chains (ParallelDivMulChainLayer):
Fragment $m/z$ ratios, neutral losses, and precursor_mz relationships carry strong physical structure. Rather than mixing all features into a single dot product, this layer routes features through 16 isolated parallel paths, each computing a rational interaction:
$$y_k = \tanh\left(\tanh\left(\frac{u_{k,1}}{\text{safe}(u_{k,2})}\right) \cdot u_{k,3} + b_k\right)$$
paired with a residual skip-connection from the raw spectral features.


Topological Metadata Tails & Shared "Non-Neuron" (SharedNonNeuron):
MS2 spectra vary heavily in peak density (num_peaks from a few peaks to 100+). Every sample carries a dynamic metadata tail tracking exact num_peaks, precursor_mz, and total intensity. A single shared Non-Neuron micro-network reads both layer statistics (mean, max, min) and the log-scaled tail to modulate fragment embeddings based on spectral richness.



📈 Iterative Progress on Public LB


v1 (0.057): 16 peaks, 20k training pairs, broad mass window (±0.05 Da), no adduct filtering.

v2 (0.089): 32 peaks + pairwise top-fragment mass differences, 120k paired inchikey14 spectra (CLIP-style symmetric contrastive loss), tight mass window (±0.015 Da), exact adduct matching, and multi-spectrum score aggregation per molecule_id.
