<div style="font-size:27px;font-weight:820;color:#FFFFFF;margin-top:9px;">When a Spectral Fingerprint Gets the Answer Wrong</div>
<div style="color:#CFE2EF;font-size:14px;line-height:1.75;margin-top:10px;">A molecular formula is not unique. Now we ask a harder question: can experimental MS/MS distinguish two isomers when the instrument, library, adduct and collision energy are carefully matched?</div>
</div>

This diagnostic set contains **10,299 experimental spectra** selected from the full `train.parquet` (2,539,608 records). Repeated measurements, isomers and cross-library matches are deliberately overrepresented, so the sample proportions **must not be interpreted as population frequencies**. Spectra truncated to the top 250 peaks are excluded from paired experiments, as documented below.

**Matching procedure:** relative peak intensities are square-root transformed and m/z peaks are matched within ±0.02 Da without reusing a peak. Comparisons across collision energies require the same structure, source, adduct and instrument. This is a spectral diagnostic experiment, not competition candidate ranking.
