<div style="font-size:14px;">
<strong style="color:#8DEDDD;">01 / A formula is not a structure.</strong> Among 51,291 molecular formulas in the full training set, <b>25,298</b> correspond to multiple distinct `inchikey14` structures.<br>
<strong style="color:#8DEDDD;">02 / Collision energy reshapes the fragments.</strong> Across 221 tightly matched structures, the median intensity share in new peaks is <b>43.0%</b> for distant-energy pairs versus <b>2.5%</b> for near-energy pairs.<br>
<strong style="color:#F8D09A;">03 / A spectral look-alike can win.</strong> In 18 of 274 two-candidate duels, cosine favored the wrong isomer. We examined a real case with the same formula, adduct, instrument, and 40 eV.<br>
<strong style="color:#F8D09A;">04 / Intuition about features must be tested.</strong> Square-root intensity weighting helped on this diagnostic panel; extra IDF weighting for rare peaks provided no net gain.<br>
<strong style="color:#8DEDDD;">05 / Errors cluster within chemical groups.</strong> The 18 wrong decisions occur in only <b>11 molecular formulas</b>. Duel-level accuracy alone is insufficient; we also need structure- and formula-level controls.
