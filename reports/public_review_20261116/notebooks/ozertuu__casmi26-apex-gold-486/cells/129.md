## Part II · From Spectral Evidence to Molecular Ranking

<div style="max-width:1020px;padding:29px 30px;border-radius:17px;background:#10243B;border:1px solid #315873;color:#E9F6FF;margin:18px 0 22px;">
<div style="font-size:11px;font-weight:850;letter-spacing:2px;color:#74E3D1;">DVORKIN RESEARCH · FROM DISCOVERY TO IDENTIFICATION</div>
<div style="font-size:26px;color:#FFFFFF;font-weight:850;margin:12px 0;line-height:1.3;">The evidence is in. Now rank the suspects.</div>
<div style="font-size:14px;color:#C9DFEE;line-height:1.75;">Part I explored why matching peaks is not enough: structural isomers, collision energy, adducts, acquisition conditions and spectral libraries can pull the evidence in different directions. Here, those clues meet a public molecular-identification system that retrieves, scores and ranks up to 25 candidate structures for each unknown.</div>

The model implementation follows [Hongbin Gu's V589 + external-library pipeline](https://www.kaggle.com/code/guhongbin/casmi26-v589-alex0484-extlib), the [0.487 scorer configuration documented by alex chilton](https://www.kaggle.com/code/alexchilton/casmi26-lb-0-487-peaks-to-structures-scorer) and the public [IONWRAITH notebook by lucifer19](https://www.kaggle.com/code/lucifer19/ionwraith-v11-evidence-guided-molecule-id/notebook?scriptVersionId=368160273). Their methods, code and model assets are credited at the end of this chapter.
