## 35 · Neutral Loss: Chemical Clue or Mass Coincidence?

Experiment 26 looked for familiar neutral losses, including H₂O and CO₂. But a chemically plausible mass difference is **not proof of a real fragmentation pathway**: as peak counts rise, chance matches also become more likely.

**Negative control.** Compare the observed fraction of intensity near known loss masses (±0.01 Da) with a decoy obtained by replacing the precursor m/z with that of another molecule from the same adduct and a nearby mass range. The measured fragment peaks stay unchanged.

This intentionally breaks the link between precursor and fragments while preserving part of the mass distribution. It is not a chemically perfect null model; it asks whether observed matches exceed a simple background.
